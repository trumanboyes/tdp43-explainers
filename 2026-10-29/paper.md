# Paper

**Salivary Dim-Light Melatonin Onset is Associated with Functional Decline, Respiratory Symptom Emergence, and Survival in Early Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis**
Bombaci A, Iadarola A, Giraudo A, Fattori E, Sinagra S, Magnino A, Falbo M, De Marco G, Calvo A, Chiò A, Cicolin A (Turin ALS Center, "Rita Levi Montalcini" Department of Neuroscience, University of Torino, Italy, and collaborators)
*Annals of Neurology* 2026. Published online Oct 16, 2026.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78398
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42885660/

## One-line note
A prospective longitudinal observational study of 50 people with ALS within 18 months of symptom onset. Each did home-based salivary melatonin sampling under dim light at 6 time points around habitual sleep onset; dim-light melatonin onset (DLMO) was the time a fitted spline first reached 3 pg/ml. Age- and sex-matched controls were also profiled. ALS participants had an earlier DLMO than controls (20:24 ± 1:18 vs 20:58 ± 0:50; p = 0.028) despite similar habitual sleep onset and chronotype. Within the ALS cohort, a later baseline DLMO correlated with worse functional/motor status, faster progression, incident dyspnea/orthopnea by 6 months (adjusted OR 3.11; p = 0.019), and poorer survival/tracheostomy-free outcome. In 28 people resampled at 6 months, DLMO and melatonin metrics did not change. The authors describe the prognostic associations as exploratory and call for larger independent cohorts. The study did not test melatonin as a treatment. Summary based on the PubMed abstract only; the full text is not open access and was not read.
