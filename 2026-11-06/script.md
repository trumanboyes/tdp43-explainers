# Script: TMEM106B C-terminal fragment worsens TDP-43 and α-synuclein toxicity

*Narrated by ElevenLabs (voice: Carolyn). Plain-English explainer, not medical advice.*

Hi. Tonight's paper is a bioRxiv preprint posted October 30, 2026, from Ruben Riordan, Aileen Saxton, Marina Han, Kailee Parrott, and Brian Kraemer at the University of Washington and VA Puget Sound. The title is: "TMEM106B C-Terminal fragment aggregation exacerbates alpha-synuclein and TDP-43 proteinopathy phenotypes." It is a U.S. Government work released under a CC0 license.

The question. Genetic variants in TMEM106B raise risk for frontotemporal dementia driven by TDP-43, and related dementias. A piece of the protein called the C-terminal fragment, or TMEM C-T, aggregates in many neurodegenerative diseases, including ALS and FTLD-TDP. The team asked: when that fragment aggregates alongside hallmark disease proteins, does it make the damage worse?

What they did. They used low-expressing TMEM C-T strains of the roundworm C. elegans that, on their own, show little or no neuronal dysfunction. They crossed those worms with models of tau, amyloid-beta, polyQ40, alpha-synuclein, or TDP-43. Then they measured neuronal function, neuron loss, and how much of each pathogenic protein piled up.

What they found. The strongest synergy was with TDP-43 and with alpha-synuclein. Worms carrying both TMEM C-T and TDP-43 had worse locomotion, more GABAergic neuron loss, and mutually elevated accumulation: more insoluble TMEM C-T, and more total and phosphorylated TDP-43. The same mutual boost showed up with alpha-synuclein: more insoluble TMEM C-T and more insoluble alpha-synuclein, plus much worse motor function. Synergy with tau was milder: function and neuron loss got worse, but the proteins did not clearly raise each other's levels. There was little or no interaction with amyloid-beta.

What it means. In roughly ninety-seven percent of ALS, including most people without a known gene mutation, TDP-43 pathology is central. This worm study suggests secondary TMEM C-T co-pathology can amplify TDP-43-driven toxicity and may blunt therapies that only hit the primary protein. It is not a treatment, and it does not prove the same synergy in people.

Honest caveats. This is a preprint, not yet peer-reviewed. The model is C. elegans, not a human trial. Low TMEM C-T alone looked largely silent; the damage appeared when it co-occurred with other proteinopathies. This summary is based on the full preprint PDF.

What's next. The authors call for more work on how TMEM C-T interacts with TDP-43 and alpha-synuclein in human disease, and for considering secondary co-pathology when designing therapies. This is educational only, not medical advice. Talk with your care team about your own situation.
