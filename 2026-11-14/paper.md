## One-line note
Single-centre (Gold Coast University Hospital, Australia), randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase II trial of a cannabis-based medicine extract (MediCabilis CBD oil: 50 mg CBD and <2 mg THC per ml; titrated over 14 days from 50 mg CBD up to a maximum of 350 mg CBD / <14 mg THC per day as tolerated) versus hemp seed oil placebo for 6 months. Recruitment ran Nov 2018–Mar 2023; at least 100 clinic patients were pre-screened but only 17 were enrolled (CBME 8, placebo 9) against a target of 38, and the DSMB closed the study early. Barriers: COVID-19, a mandatory driving ban (THC), a change of drug supplier, and over-the-counter low-dose CBD in Australia from 2022. 16/17 had sporadic ALS. 9/17 did not reach end of treatment (5 deaths: 4 placebo, 1 CBME; 2 palliative care; 1 increasing disability; 1 emigrated), leaving 8 (4 per group) for the primary analysis. Primary outcome (adjusted mean ALSFRS-R at 6 months): 31.67 CBME vs 34.33 placebo, difference −2.66 (95% CI −7.25 to 1.92; p=0.18). No significant differences in FVC, spasticity, pain, quality of life, weight loss or total ECAS; post hoc ALSFRS-R analyses (imputed, month 3, mixed effects) also showed no effect. Adverse events 33 CBME vs 29 placebo; serious AEs 1 vs 4. Post hoc, exploratory: usual pain 2.24 vs 4.76 (−2.52; 95% CI −8.92 to 3.87; p=0.34), ECAS executive +7.71 (95% CI 0.90 to 14.52; p=0.04), memory +5.68 (p=0.06). Authors conclude the trial was underpowered and did not show an effect on ALS progression; pain and cognition findings are hypothesis-generating only. Conflicts: one author advises a company developing a cannabis-based ALS drug (unrelated product); another previously received honoraria from the study-drug supplier.
