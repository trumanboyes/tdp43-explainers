# Paper

**Human Endogenous Retrovirus K RNA triggers cytoplasmic TDP-43 aggregation by initiating pathological nuclear condensates**

Chang Y-H, Dubnau J (Department of Anesthesiology, Stony Brook School of Medicine; Department of Neurobiology and Behavior, Stony Brook University, NY). *bioRxiv* preprint, posted November 17, 2026 — **a preprint, not peer reviewed**, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.16.767080
- Full text / PDF: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.16.767080v1.full
- Funding: National Institute on Aging (R01AG078788, R01AG076493). Competing interests declared: J. Dubnau is on the scientific advisory board of Transposon Therapeutics, but states no funding support was received from the company.

## What the study did
- Laboratory work in human cell lines only (no people, no patient tissue). Main model: HEK293T cells in which the cell's own (endogenous) TDP-43 gene is CRISPR-tagged with HaloTag, so TDP-43 can be followed in fixed and live cells.
- HERV-K was switched on in two ways: (1) transfecting HERV-K constructs — wild type, a version with the primer binding site deleted (HERV-KΔPBS, which cannot make first-strand cDNA), and H2B-mCherry as a negative control; (2) treating cells with the DNA methyltransferase inhibitor 5-azacytidine (500 or 1000 nM, 72 h) to reactivate the cells' own epigenetically silenced HERV-K.
- Readouts at 72 and 120 hours after transfection: confocal imaging of Halo-tagged TDP-43 and phosphorylated TDP-43 (pTDP-43), live-cell and super-resolution imaging, RT-qPCR of cryptic-exon inclusion in STMN2 and UNC13A, RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) with a pTDP-43 antibody, soluble/insoluble fractionation, and cell-death assays.
- Also repeated in human microglial (HMC3), astrocyte (SVG p12) and neuroblastoma (SH-SY5Y) lines, in HEK293T cells with no Halo tag, and in Halo-GAPDH cells as a tagging control; plus Drosophila S2 cells with the fly ERV mdg4.

## Key results (verified against the preprint text)
- HERV-K or HERV-KΔPBS expression produced cytoplasmic TDP-43 aggregates (Halo- and pTDP-43-positive) by 72 h, still present at 120 h; control cells showed almost none. Deleting the PBS did not abolish the effect (it was, if anything, stronger), so cDNA synthesis is not required.
- Spherical nuclear structures appeared, with pTDP-43 and Halo-TDP-43 on their outer surface — named PETANS (Pathological ERV-TDP-43-Associated Nuclear Spheres); seen at 72 h and more numerous at 120 h.
- Cryptic-exon inclusion in STMN2 and UNC13A rose over time with HERV-K expression (greater with HERV-KΔPBS), i.e. loss of normal nuclear TDP-43 function.
- Live imaging and super-resolution microscopy: PETANS form dynamically in the nucleus and some exit into the cytoplasm to form large TDP-43 inclusions; those cytoplasmic aggregates were enriched for nuclear pore complex and Lamin B1 proteins.
- PETANS are not anisosomes (no Hsp70 enrichment or redistribution) and partially overlap nucleolar (fibrillarin) and Cajal body markers. Cytoplasmic aggregates associated with G3BP1 early and with p62 later; TDP-43 shifted from the soluble to the insoluble fraction; cell death increased in Halo-TDP-43 and SH-SY5Y cells.
- Reactivating the cells' own HERV-K with 5-azacytidine reproduced PETANS and cytoplasmic aggregation and increased STMN2 cryptic-exon inclusion (UNC13A not significantly). Dicer-substrate siRNAs against HERV-K reduced both the transcripts and the pathology.
- The trigger is the RNA, not the proteins: none of the individual HERV-K ORFs reproduced the effect (Gag modestly increased cytoplasmic aggregation but not PETANS), and a construct in which Gag translation was blocked by premature stop codons was just as potent. A non-coding construct containing only the two LTRs (HERV-KΔPBS-RNAt) was as effective as the full element.
- The minimal element is the R region of the LTR, which contains a UG-rich putative TDP-43 binding domain (TBD). Deleting that TBD greatly reduced PETANS and cytoplasmic aggregation and abolished the RIP interaction between HERV-K RNA with pathological TDP-43. The TBD is specific to HERV-K among HERV families. The human genome has roughly 100 HERV-K proviruses with coding ORFs and about 1,000 solo LTR copies, all of which carry the R element.
- Parallel experiments with the Drosophila mdg4-ERV gave the same pattern (RNA, not ORFs, drives TDP-43 pathology).

## Caveats
- Preprint — not peer reviewed.
- Immortalized cell lines with HERV-K driven to high levels (or pharmacologically de-silenced); not neurons or tissue from people with ALS. The authors state it will be important to confirm PETANS in human postmortem brain and in animal models.
- Cross-sectional cell experiments cannot establish what happens in a human nervous system over years, and 5-azacytidine de-silences many elements, not only HERV-K (the siRNA experiments address, but do not completely remove, that concern).
