## One-line note
Sonoelectromyography study: supramaximal nerve stimulation with simultaneous CMAP recording through a ring electrode and continuous-wave Doppler ultrasound at the same site, in 28 people with ALS (25 classic ALS including 3 with progressive bulbar palsy, plus 3 with PLS; mean ALSFRS-R 34 ± 8, median duration 16 months), 29 age-matched controls and 27 younger controls (21–29 y). Electromechanical delay (EMD = mechanical latency − distal motor latency) was measured bilaterally in abductor digiti minimi, brachioradialis, tibialis anterior and rectus femoris. EMD was significantly longer in ALS than age-matched controls in all four muscles (ALS 6.9–15.7 ms vs 4.8–10.5 ms; tibialis anterior 11.07 ± 2.8 vs 6.29 ms). Tibialis anterior alone gave AUC 0.906; a combination of the four largest of eight EMD values gave AUC 0.903 with sensitivity 84.6% and specificity 92.6%. In a linear mixed model (log EMD ~ group + age + sex + muscle + patient), ALS differed from age-matched controls (p < 0.0001) but not significantly from younger controls (p = 0.210); age and sex were not significant. EMD did not correlate with ALSFRS-R, disease duration, cramps, fasciculations, leg atrophy or reflexes, and PLS did not differ from the rest of the cohort. DML was mildly prolonged (max ~116% for TA); DML–mechanical latency correlations were small to moderate, suggesting EMD adds information beyond conduction latency. Two patients could not be tested and several participants stopped early because stimulation was painful (mostly rectus femoris). Limitations: small single-center cohort, single investigator, healthy controls only (no ALS-mimic disease controls such as multifocal motor neuropathy), cutoffs not validated, temperature and limb position not strictly standardized, no genetic data reported.
