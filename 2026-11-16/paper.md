# Paper

**Power-law age-dependence supports phenotype-specific multistep architecture in MND**

Vucic S, Henderson RD, Schultz D, Needham M, Mathers S, McCombe PA, Talman P, Kiernan MC (University of Sydney Brain and Nerve Research Center, Royal Brisbane and Women's Hospital, Flinders, Fiona Stanley/Perron/Murdoch, Calvary Bethlehem/Monash, Deakin/Geelong, Neuroscience Research Australia/UNSW; Australian Motor Neuron Disease Registry). *Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis and Frontotemporal Degeneration*, published online November 14, 2026 (received Jul 25, accepted Nov 8, 2026) — peer reviewed, open access (CC BY 4.0). PMID 43033081.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/21678421.2026.2762732
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033081/
- Funding: none reported. Conflicts: SV and MCK are co-inventors of MagXite software; SV is director of a company holding equity in Clene Nanomedicine and receives Biogen advisory fees (see paper for full statement).

## What the study did
- Data: curated Australian Motor Neuron Disease Registry (AMNDR), clinical and onset data 2005–2016, contributed by 10 major Australian MND clinical sites.
- Primary analysis: 2,556 people with symptom onset at ages 30–74; mean onset age 59.7 years (SD 9.9), median 61.0; mean disease duration 25.8 months. Median 17 months from symptom onset to registry enrollment.
- Cases counted in 5-year age-at-onset bands, with Australian population denominators as offsets in Poisson regression. Two models compared: power law (log cases vs log age; apparent steps = slope + 1, the Armitage–Doll multistep framework) versus exponential (log cases vs age), using AIC/BIC.
- Motivation: a recent analysis (Foffani and colleagues) argued that ALS incidence is better described by exponential age-dependence, challenging the multistep model.

## Key results (verified against the full text)
- Total cohort: power-law AIC 101.48 vs exponential 161.54 (ΔAIC 60.06; ΔBIC also 60.06) in favour of the power law; McFadden pseudo-R² 0.95.
- Power-law slope 3.75 (95% CI 3.55–3.95) → about 4.75 apparent steps (95% CI 4.55–4.95). The exponential fit implied a doubling every 10.19 years but fit worse.
- By onset site: bulbar slope 5.01 (≈6.01 steps), lumbar 3.51 (≈4.51 steps), cervical 2.66 (≈3.66 steps). Power law was also favoured in flail arm, flail leg, and PLS (smaller margins; fewer cases). Highest step estimates in bulbar onset and flail arm; lowest in cervical onset and PLS.
- Sensitivity analyses: power law still better across ages 20–90 (lower step estimates) and in a restricted younger range (<60 / 25–59 years), and in a Foffani-style R² comparison.

## Caveats (from the authors)
- Curve fitting is compatible with, but cannot prove, a multistep process; it does not identify the steps or imply every patient goes through the same ones.
- Registry is not a complete national count (referral bias, diagnostic delay, incomplete capture), and age at onset relies on recall.
- No systematic genetic testing data, so no genotype-stratified analysis.
