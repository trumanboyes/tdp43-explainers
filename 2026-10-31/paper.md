## One-line note
Two-cohort ante-mortem study of baseline CSF α-synuclein seed amplification assay (αSyn SAA, RT-QuIC, 4 replicate wells, one lab in Bologna) in 443 people with ALS (Nagoya n = 248; Bologna n = 195), compared with BioFINDER-2 participants (cognitively unimpaired n = 999, Aβ-positive MCI n = 224, AD dementia n = 319). Strong positivity (≥3/4 wells on first run) was found in 24/443 (5.4%); overall positivity including weak positives (confirmed on repeat runs) was 37/443 (8.4%). Age- and sex-adjusted odds of positivity in ALS were not higher than in Aβ-negative cognitively unimpaired people (OR 1.33, 95% CI 0.82–2.14) and were lower than in AD dementia (OR 0.44, 0.27–0.70), suggesting largely age-related co-pathology. In sporadic ALS, strong positivity was associated with shorter tracheostomy-free survival (median 14 vs 31 months, log-rank P = 0.019; adjusted HR 2.08 [1.25–3.45] after age, ΔFS, %FVC and CSF NfL), and modified the NfL–ALSFRS-R decline relationship (NfL × αSyn SAA × time β −0.70 points/month per SD NfL [−1.26, −0.14]); its main effect on ALSFRS-R slope was not significant (β −0.24, P = 0.16). Weak positivity showed no clear survival association; weak-positive patients were older, had longer disease duration and more Aβ positivity. Results held when familial/genetic cases were included and in Aβ-negative patients. Autopsy in 5 Nagoya cases matched the SAA classification (one strong-positive case with Braak LB stage 6 Lewy pathology plus TDP-43 Brettschneider stage 4). Limitations: preprint; few strong-positive cases (low power) and need for independent validation; strong/weak grading not yet validated for prognosis; no systematic assessment of Lewy body features or DAT imaging; neuropathology in only 5 people.
