## One-line note
Baseline CSF from 264 people with ALS (Nagoya prospective cohort, 2013–2024; 16 with a known pathogenic variant, 69 not genetically tested) and 164 disease controls (71 ALS mimics plus CIDP, MSA, PD, iNPH), measuring NfL, GFAP, Aβ38/40/42 and pTau181/pTau217. ALS had higher NfL, Aβ38, Aβ40, Aβ42, Aβ38/40, pTau181 and pTau217 than controls, even in Aβ-negative subgroups; GFAP and Aβ42/40 were similar. NfL alone discriminated ALS from controls with AUC 0.85 (0.82–0.88); random forest/XGBoost models adding other markers reached 0.90 (0.88–0.92), and a two-cut-off NfL “grey zone” strategy kept overall PPV around 0.90 (grey-zone AUC about 0.77). Aβ positivity (CSF Aβ42/40 < 0.077) was 14% (37/264) in ALS vs 13% in controls, with largely preserved cognition (median MMSE 27). Aβ-related measures did not predict progression on their own but modified NfL’s association with ALSFRS-R decline (n = 218) and survival; pooled with an independent Bologna cohort (n = 153), NfL HR for death/tracheostomy was 4.02 (2.17–7.45) in Aβ-positive vs 1.62 (1.36–1.92) in Aβ-negative ALS. Models with NfL plus clinical features predicted individual slopes only modestly (cross-validated R² about 0.20); NfL-based enrichment cut simulated trial sample sizes by 21–34%. Limitations: observational, mostly single-centre Japanese cohort; small Aβ-positive subgroup; no external validation of the diagnostic models; CSF requires lumbar puncture; high ALS prevalence in the sample inflates PPV; trial simulations illustrative only. Several authors report industry fees (declared as outside the submitted work). Summary based on the full text (open-access article on thelancet.com; supplementary appendix not read).
