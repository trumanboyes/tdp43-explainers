## One-line note
Multi-cohort study of T cell immunity in blood and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) in ALS. Cohort A: paired blood/CSF single-cell RNA + TCR sequencing in 20 people with sporadic ALS sampled at diagnosis (before riluzole), compared with public CSF data from 20 age/sex-matched healthy controls. Putative antigen-presenting cell (APC)–T cell complexes were enriched in ALS CSF and carried clonally expanded T cells; imaging flow cytometry (cohort B, n = 4) confirmed CD3+CD14+ complexes, most with focal ICAM1 suggestive of (not necessarily mature) immunological synapses. Clonal expansion was increased in ALS but highly variable between patients and did not correlate with progression rate. Longitudinal single-cell data (cohort C, n = 4, 1 year apart): ~17% of clonotypes persisted overall, and 96% of large CSF clonotypes (>5 cells) remained detectable. Flow cytometry cohort D (n = 159 Swedish patients, 44 with multiple time points): T cell frequencies peaked ~1 year after diagnosis; higher blood Treg and effector T cell proportions were associated with faster ALSFRS-R decline, whereas higher levels of all T cell subsets in CSF correlated with slower progression; higher blood CD8+ T cells predicted 6-month mortality. Shared blood/CSF clonotypes showed compartment-specific states (CSF: migration, tissue residency, lipid metabolism; blood: terminal differentiation, cytotoxicity). Database-inferred CMV-specific T cells in CSF (not blood; not EBV) correlated with faster progression; CMV-specific T cell numbers were similar in ALS and controls, and CMV transcripts in postmortem frontal cortex (cohort E, n = 79) were detected at similar rates in ALS and controls. In a Finnish serology cohort (cohort F, n = 706), higher anti-CMV IE1 IgG was associated with shorter survival only in patients without a known genetic cause (opposite direction in C9orf72 carriers). Limitations: TCR antigen specificity inferred from databases; small single-cell and longitudinal cohorts; healthy-control comparisons rely on public datasets; possible technical doublets; all associations are correlative, not causal. Summary based on the full text (Nature Medicine open-access article).
