# Paper

**Circulating gelsolin tracks disease stage in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis across four cohorts**

## What the study did
- Sheffield arm of the AMBRoSIA study: serum proteome (Olink Explore HT, 5,401 assays) in 118 people with MND (94 with ALS) and 24 controls; plasma metabolome (1,659 metabolites) and CSF metabolome (513 metabolites) in subsets.
- Defined "disease stage" in deceased patients as the fraction of the onset-to-death interval elapsed at sampling (0 = onset, 1 = death); separately used the D50 model (from ALSFRS-R scores) for "aggressiveness" (speed) and "phase" (position).
- Replication in Tel Aviv (serum mass spectrometry; 349 ALS, 290 deceased), Oxford (serum Olink; 381 patients, 260 deceased; 222 with repeated samples) and AnswerALS (plasma SomaScan; 129 deceased, 431 samples); ageing comparisons with UK Biobank proteomics.

## Key results
- In Sheffield, 199 proteins were nominally linked to survival; only NfL passed multiple-testing correction (HR per SD 2.67). These survival-linked proteins were strongly enriched for stage associations (odds ratio 5.1 in Sheffield, 2.9 Oxford, 1.6 Tel Aviv).
- 68 proteins tracked stage in at least two of four datasets (31 higher, 37 lower at later stage); 61 of 68 changed within individuals over time in the same direction. NfL was not a stage marker.
- The stage signature linked to higher complement C4/C3, C-reactive protein and ferritin, and lower creatine kinase (inflammation and muscle loss), but not to body weight or NfL. Proteins rising with stage also rose with age in UK Biobank (17 of 17); proteomic age gap rose nominally with stage (r = 0.25, P = 0.027; BH-adjusted P = 0.055).
- Gelsolin (GSN) had the strongest, most consistent stage association across all four cohorts (partial r about -0.27 to -0.32; 7-10% of variance in stage) and fell within individuals over time. In the D50 analysis (170 Oxford participants) gelsolin tracked phase (beta -0.41) but not aggressiveness (-0.08, P = 0.25); NfL tracked aggressiveness (0.56) but not phase (0.13, P = 0.056). Gelsolin explained 19% of the variance in phase.
- Survival prediction (Sheffield + Oxford, 475 participants, 336 deaths; cross-validated C-index): 0.60 age/sex, 0.63 + gelsolin, 0.73 + NfL, 0.76 with both.

## Caveats
- Preprint, not peer reviewed.
- Disease stage can only be defined in deceased patients and includes survival time, so it partly conditions on the outcome.
- The 68-protein signature was defined without multiple-testing correction; metabolite analyses were from Sheffield only; Oxford results were returned as aggregates; the D50 analysis used a simplified model; fasting and sample-handling variables were not recorded.
- Gelsolin is a research finding, not a clinical test.
