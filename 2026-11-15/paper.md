## One-line note
Prospective longitudinal biobank study (Macquarie University Neurodegenerative Disease Biobank): serum IGF-1 measured by ELISA (blinded) in 37 people with probable/definite ALS (25 men, 12 women; mean age 66.7; mean baseline ALSFRS-R 34.9; median 0.5 years since diagnosis at baseline) and 25 age- and sex-matched controls. Baseline IGF-1 was numerically higher in ALS but not significantly different (132.2 ± 68.3 vs 107.3 ± 54.3 ng/mL; Cohen's d 0.40; p = 0.12) and did not differ across slow/normal/fast progression groups defined by disease duration (p = 0.88). Primary lagged analysis (48 visit-to-visit intervals from 23 participants; median interval 0.5 years), adjusted for interval, baseline BMI, age and sex: each 1-SD higher IGF-1 at the prior visit was associated with 1.40 points less ALSFRS-R decline at the next visit (95% CI 0.03 to 2.77; p = 0.045); similar in GEE (1.50; p = 0.030) and when adjusting for prior ALSFRS-R (1.45; p = 0.036), but not significant under conservative small-cluster (CR2) inference (p = 0.084 and 0.071). IGF-1 was fairly stable within people (ICC 0.69); the between-person component, not visit-to-visit fluctuation, carried the association; it stayed positive in all 23 leave-one-out analyses (range 0.95–1.86). In the better-fitting random-slope mixed model the IGF-1 × time effect was positive but not significant (β 1.86 points/year per SD; p = 0.254). Prior ALSFRS-R did not significantly predict later IGF-1 (p = 0.096). Exploratory spline/cutpoint/classification analyses were inconclusive (spline p = 0.19; no robust threshold; no improvement in classifying clinically meaningful decline). No genetic data reported. Authors frame IGF-1 as a possible marker of systemic "trophic-metabolic reserve," not a standalone prognostic biomarker or proof that raising IGF-1 would help; prior trials of recombinant IGF-1 in ALS did not show clear benefit. Limitations: small complete-case samples, single centre, no external validation, no measures of muscle mass, nutrition, IGF-binding proteins or downstream signalling.
