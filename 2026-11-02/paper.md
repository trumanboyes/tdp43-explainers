## One-line note
Cross-sectional analysis of a prospective multicenter observational study (15 ALS centers in Germany and Austria, recruited Apr–Dec 2025; 1,307 people with ALS by Gold Coast criteria; mean onset age 60.7, 59% male; median ALSFRS-R 33, median progression rate 0.42 points/month). Neurologists applied the ALS-OPM classification (v3.1): Onset region (head, distal/proximal arm, trunk respiratory/axial, distal/proximal leg), Propagation (early = spread to a vertically distant body region within 12 months [43.4%] vs later [52.5%]), and Motor neuron dysfunction (balanced UMN/LMN 42.6%, dominant/pure UMN, dominant/pure LMN, dissociated). Classification frequencies were consistent across centers (Kendall's W 0.75–0.89). Early propagation was the strongest marker of aggressiveness: faster ALSFRS-R progression, higher serum NfL (Simoa), and shorter survival (age/sex-adjusted HR 2.93, 95% CI 2.18–3.93). Among similarly fast progressors, early-propagation patients had higher NfL. Trunk (especially respiratory) onset was rare (~3%) but progressed fastest with the lowest NfL-to-progression index; head onset had the highest NfL and higher mortality than several limb-onset groups; proximal leg onset had the slowest progression and most favorable survival. Balanced UMN/LMN had the fastest progression and highest NfL; LMN phenotypes had higher mortality than UMN phenotypes (HR 1.96); the pure-UMN group had no deaths. Limitations: short follow-up in a tertiary-center cohort (referral/survivorship bias), small rare-phenotype groups, axes analyzed separately (not mutually adjusted), phenotyping based on history/exam (recall bias, interrater variability), no cognitive/behavioral assessment; associations only. Some authors are founders/employees of APST, which makes the "ALS App" used for remote ALSFRS-R collection. Summary based on the full text (open-access article via PMC).
