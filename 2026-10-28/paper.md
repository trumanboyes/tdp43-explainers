# Paper

**Plasma Proteomic Signatures Predict Incident Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis: A Prospective Study Stratified by C9orf72 Expansion**
Su W-M, Duan Q-Q, Zhang N, He S-Y, Liu R-Y, Wen X-J, Gu X-J, Wang Y, Cao B, Chen Y-P (Department of Neurology and Institute of Neurology, West China Hospital, Sichuan University, Chengdu, China)
*Journal of Cachexia, Sarcopenia and Muscle* 2026;17(5):e70405. Published online Oct 20, 2026. Open access (CC BY 4.0).

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/jcsm.70405
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13683052/
- PubMed: 42901869

## One-line note
A prospective UK Biobank study. The authors measured 2,920 plasma proteins (Olink Explore) in blood drawn in 2006–2010 from 52,584 people without ALS, then followed them for a mean of 11.8 years. During follow-up, 291 people developed ALS: 257 without and 34 with a C9orf72 repeat expansion, determined from whole-genome sequencing. Cox models adjusted for age, sex, ethnicity, deprivation, BMI, smoking, alcohol, fasting time and sample age. After FDR correction, nine proteins were linked to later ALS. Higher levels carried higher risk for NEFL (neurofilament light; HR 2.90 per SD), MEGF10 (1.74), THBS4 (1.57), MYBPC1 (1.35), MYOM3 (1.29) and CSRP3 (1.23). Higher levels carried lower risk for ITGAV (0.28), EGFLAM (0.40) and CD248 (0.57). In ALS without C9orf72, four proteins were significant: NEFL (HR 2.74), MYBPC1, MYOM3 and CSRP3. For C9orf72 ALS, NEFL (main text HR 4.04; the abstract says 4.40) and COL5A1 (3.07) were significant. Among 128 expansion carriers, only NEFL predicted who later developed ALS. Models combining the proteins with age and sex predicted ALS within 3, 5 and 10 years with time-dependent AUCs of about 0.78–0.87 for all ALS (0.867 at 3 years) and 0.78–0.81 for non-C9 ALS. Only NEFL and MYBPC1 replicated for non-C9 ALS in the random half-sample used for internal validation. In a cross-sectional model, plasma NEFL began to rise about 9.3 years before diagnosis and peaked 1–2 years before diagnosis in non-C9 ALS. Higher pre-diagnosis NEFL was linked to a younger age at onset. No pre-diagnosis protein predicted survival after FDR correction in all ALS or non-C9 ALS. Many of the hits are muscle proteins (MYBPC1, MYOM3, CSRP3). Mendelian randomization gave only nominal support for MYOM3 as causal (OR 1.10, p = 0.043), and colocalization did not confirm it. Limitations: there was no independent external cohort, since validation used a random half of the same cohort. The C9orf72 group was small. Proteins were measured only once per person, so the over-time curves are cross-sectional. ALS diagnoses came from health records, the cohort was 93% White, and absolute ALS risk stays very low, so the authors call these discovery-phase findings. Summary based on the full text (PMC XML); supplementary tables and figures were not inspected.
