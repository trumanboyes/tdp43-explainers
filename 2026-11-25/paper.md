# Paper

**In silico designed small molecule targets misfolded TDP-43 to rescue function and ALS pathology**

## What the study did
- Acelot's machine-learning drug-design platform (RESOLUTE) was used to design and screen small molecules that bind misfolded TDP-43; lead compound **ACE-339**.
- Tested on recombinant TDP-43 aggregates and on TDP-43 aggregates seeded from post-mortem ALS brain tissue; molecular simulations of binding.
- Cell work: stressed human cell lines and human iPSC-derived motor neurons (TDP-43 aggregation, nuclear localisation, cryptic splicing of STMN2 and UNC13A, synaptic function).
- Pharmacokinetics in wild-type mice (oral, once daily, 50/150/400 mg/kg for 14 days; brain penetrant, well tolerated).
- Two TDP-43 mouse models: iTDP43 (75 and 150 mg/kg/day oral, weeks 4–12) and the more severe rNLS8 model (150 and 400 mg/kg/day oral from DOX withdrawal at 5 weeks; n = 10 females + 10 males per group).

## Key results
- ACE-339 disaggregated TDP-43 aggregates in vitro without disrupting natively folded proteins.
- In cells it reduced TDP-43 aggregates, restored nuclear TDP-43, and reduced TDP-43-dependent mis-splicing (STMN2, UNC13A) and synaptic dysfunction.
- iTDP43 mice: reduced pathological TDP-43 and neuroinflammation; improved motor/behavioural measures (some measures not statistically significant).
- rNLS8 mice: dose-dependent delay of onset and improved survival — all vehicle mice died by day 63; high-dose median survival extended by 11.5 days (~28%, P<0.01), some mice to day 145; plasma neurofilament light reduced after 4 weeks of treatment.
- Authors state a catalogue of TDP-43 disaggregating compounds is being developed toward clinical trials in ALS and other TDP-43 proteinopathies.

## Caveats
- Preprint, not peer reviewed; written by the company developing the compound (commercial interest).
- All evidence is preclinical (in vitro, cells, mice), often at high doses; overexpression mouse models do not fully mimic human ALS.
- Mouse survival benefits often fail to translate to people; no human safety or efficacy data yet.
