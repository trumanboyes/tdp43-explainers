# Paper

## One-line note
Retrospective four-centre European study (serum from 139 ALS, 101 AD continuum, 75 disease controls, 22 healthy controls; CSF in subsets) testing why blood p-tau217, a leading Alzheimer's test, is also raised in ALS. With direct commercial Simoa assays, serum brain-derived (BD-)tau and p-tau217 were higher in both AD and ALS than controls (median serum p-tau217 0.75 AD, 0.37 ALS, 0.15 controls pg/mL), but CSF BD-tau was not raised in ALS (ALS vs controls p = 0.794) and CSF t-tau/BD-tau rises in ALS were driven by the minority with Alzheimer's co-pathology (ALS A+, 12 of 116 with CSF). After a selective BD-tau immunoprecipitation step (antibody 2B8 at the exon 4–5 junction; semi-automated, n = 149: 50 ALS, 60 AD, 39 controls), BD-tau and BD-p-tau217 were elevated only in AD, with no ALS-vs-control difference; AD-vs-ALS AUC rose from 0.729 to 0.900 for p-tau217, and the BD-tau/BD-p-tau217 ratio reached 0.961. Targeted mass spectrometry of frontal cortex (16), spinal cord (11) and muscle (10) showed a central-to-peripheral shift from BD-tau to "big tau" (exon 4a-containing) predominance, and in muscle (4 ALS, 4 controls) only big p-tau217 was detected. Serum BD-tau by the direct assay correlated with ALSFRS-R (r = −0.471) and progression rate (r = 0.318), but these links disappeared after BD-Tau-IP, suggesting they reflect muscle tau rather than neuron damage. Authors propose a two-level algorithm (direct p-tau217 first, BD-p-tau217 as confirmatory where muscle/nerve disease may confound) and suggest big-p-tau assays could someday track denervation in ALS. Limitations: retrospective; smaller two-step and CSF subsets; small, unpaired tissue sets (preliminary); slightly different CSF cut-offs across centres; no renal function data; absolute recovery after IP unknown; mass-spec method not validated in biofluids; two authors are employees of Roboscreen GmbH, which supplies the 2B8 antibody/IP.
