## One-line note
The authors tested MitoCarta3.0 nuclear-encoded mitochondrial gene pathways (1,136 genes) for ALS genetic risk with MAGMA, using the largest European ALS GWAS (27,205 cases, 110,881 controls), and analysed >1,500 bulk RNA-seq samples (KCL Brain Bank and NYGC ALS Consortium post-mortem motor cortex and cervical/lumbar spinal cord; Answer ALS iPSC-derived motor neurons). Of seven top-level categories only the mitochondrial central dogma was enriched for ALS risk (Padj = 0.044), localising to translation (Padj = 0.008) and the mitoribosome (Padj = 0.014); a nominal ROS/glutathione signal was largely due to SOD1. Directions were gene-specific (lower predicted MRPS33 and higher MRPL28 expression with higher risk), and across all MitoCarta genes genetically predicted and observed tissue expression changes agreed in direction only ~48–52% of the time (chance level). Mitochondrial genes were broadly more highly expressed in ALS motor cortex (largely a global shift; OXPHOS and mitoribosome beyond it in ALS Consortium cortex, OXPHOS in iPSC motor neurons), with no genome-wide enrichment in spinal cord. Activity clusters were nominally associated with survival only in lumbar cord (adjusted HR 0.69, unadjusted P = 0.013); no single pathway survived correction. Nuclear–mtDNA co-expression was more negative in ALS in all four post-mortem tissues (opposite in iPSC motor neurons). The authors caution that targeting a secondary mitochondrial change may be ineffective or counterproductive. Limitations: preprint; modest genetic signal; European-ancestry GWAS only; bulk RNA-seq with residual cell-composition confounding; end-stage post-mortem tissue; small control groups in some datasets.
