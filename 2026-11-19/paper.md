# Paper

**Safety and efficacy of daily oral edaravone (FNP122) in patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ADORE): a multicentre, double-blind, phase 3, randomised controlled trial**

van Eijk RPA, Povedano M, Van Damme P, Chiò A, McDermott CJ, Al-Chalabi A, Hardiman O, Varona C, Tarragó C, Nadal T, Albareda N, van den Berg LH, and the ADORE study group (lead: UMC Utrecht Brain Center, Utrecht, Netherlands; TRICALS). *The Lancet Neurology* 2026;25(12):1073–1083 (December 2026 issue; indexed in PubMed November 18, 2026, PMID 43051186) — **peer reviewed**. Funding: Ferrer.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00336-4
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43051186/
- Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05178810 (https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05178810)
- Note: the full article is behind the journal paywall; everything below is taken from the published abstract (PubMed) and the trial registry.

## What the study did
- Multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 3 randomised trial at 37 hospitals in 12 countries (Belgium, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, the Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Russia, Spain, Sweden, UK).
- Adults aged 18–80 with ALS, ≤24 months since symptom onset, vital capacity ≥70%, and ALSFRS-R decline of 0.35–1.50 points/month.
- Randomised 2:1 to oral edaravone (FNP122, 100 mg once daily) or placebo for 48 weeks; participants, investigators, staff, outcome assessors and sponsor all masked.
- Primary endpoint: difference in change from baseline in ALSFRS-R total score at week 48.

## Key results (from the abstract)
- Oct 18, 2021 – Nov 30, 2022: 370 screened, 313 randomised (209 edaravone, 104 placebo). Nine people from one site non-compliant with Good Clinical Practice were excluded (3 edaravone, 6 placebo); one per group never treated → 302 treated and analysed (205 vs 97).
- Median age 59 (IQR 53–68); 61% male; 91% White.
- Week-48 ALSFRS-R change: −12.6 (SE 0.7) edaravone vs −13.4 (SE 1.0) placebo; difference 0.78 points (95% CI −1.44 to 3.00), p = 0.49 — not significant.
- Treatment-emergent adverse events 88% vs 85%; no deaths considered treatment-related.
- Authors' interpretation: well tolerated but did not slow functional decline; results do not support use at the tested dose for modifying disease progression in the general ALS population.

## Caveats
- Summary based on the abstract only (full text paywalled).
- FNP122 is a distinct oral formulation, and the trial design differs from the studies behind existing edaravone approvals (IV edaravone and the oral suspension), so results are not directly interchangeable.
- 48-week duration; mostly White European participants; one site's participants excluded.
