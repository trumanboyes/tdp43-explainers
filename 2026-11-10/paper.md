## One-line note
The ALS-Reproducible Antibody Platform (ALS-RAP) prioritized 33 ALS-associated proteins and benchmarked 303 antibodies (297 commercial, 6 new recombinant scFv antibodies made by the Structural Genomics Consortium) using knockout-based workflows from the YCharOS open-science initiative (wild-type vs. matched knockout cells; VCP by siRNA). Four genes (SOD1, TARDBP, C9orf72, FUS) account for >80% of ALS-specific publications (10,809 of 13,173); the other 29 form the poorly characterized "dark ALS-ome". Overall, 243/303 antibodies (80%) were specific in western blot, 105/238 (44%) in immunoprecipitation and 107/231 (46%) in immunofluorescence; at least one renewable, knockout-validated antibody was found for 32 (97%), 23 (70%) and 20 (61%) proteins in those three uses. The new scFv against PFN1 was the only one of 16 tested that immunoprecipitated it. With the best antibodies, 30 proteins were profiled across human iPSC-derived motor neurons, dopaminergic neurons, oligodendrocytes, astrocytes and microglia plus primary fetal astrocytes, fetal microglia and macrophages: many ALS proteins were detected in glial and immune cells, with ACSL5, ANXA11, C9orf72 and CHCHD10 higher in microglia, CAV1 in astrocytes, and ATXN2, KIF5A, TUBA4A and VAPB mainly in neurons; RNA and protein patterns agreed for 16 of 27 proteins. Characterization reports for all 33 proteins are on Zenodo and the Only Good Antibodies site. eLife assessment: "important" and "solid", with the caveat that validated reagents still need checking in each lab's own protocol. Limitations: cultured cells from healthy-control iPSC lines, not ALS patient tissue; no disease mechanism or treatment tested. Summary based on the full article text (Europe PMC full-text XML).
