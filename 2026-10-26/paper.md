**CCT2 expression marks a translation-activated state in sporadic ALS motor neurons and is associated with functional decline**

## One-line note
Analysis of iPSC-derived motor neurons from the Answer ALS cohort (644 RNA-seq samples from 619 participants: 391 sporadic ALS, 43 C9orf72, 12 SOD1, 173 controls), with matched proteomics (436 participants) and whole-genome sequencing (360 sporadic patients). C9orf72 and SOD1 motor neurons did not converge on a shared signature. Scoring sporadic patients on three pre-specified axes (metabolic, cell cycle, inflammation) gave three states: S-ALS-1 (85 patients, 22%, pan-activated, largely confounded by differentiation quality), S-ALS-2 (204, 52%, quiescent) and S-ALS-3 (102 patients, ~26%, high metabolic and cell-cycle/MYC-target scores). CCT2 alone separated S-ALS-3 from S-ALS-2 (patient-level AUC 0.892, 95% CI 0.851–0.927) and was higher at the protein level (Wilcoxon P = 0.033); a composite of all eight TRiC subunits did not differ. Upstream regulator analysis suggested MYC and mTORC1 activation. No germline signal explained the states (no damaging CCT2 variants, null cis-eQTL, no rare-variant burden difference). CCT2 associated with faster ALSFRS-R decline (beta −0.137 points/month per SD, P = 0.005) but this weakened and lost significance after adjusting for culture composition; the NfL association could not be separated from subtype; disease duration did not differ and survival was not adequately testable; no association survived correction in a 23-endpoint clinical screen. In postmortem frontal cortex (Visium spatial data, layer 5b-enriched spots), CCT2 tracked the cell-cycle axis. Limitations: preprint; variable culture purity; no independent iPSC cohort replication; limited proteomics depth; mechanism inferred from expression only.
