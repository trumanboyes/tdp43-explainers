## One-line note
Retrospective case–control online questionnaire (Sep 2020–Mar 2024): 475 people with ALS (about 4% reported a genetic cause; median 2 years since symptom onset) and 285 controls (mostly relatives, partners, friends, caregivers), asking about the 10 years before symptom onset. People with ALS reported more prodromal complaints on an open question (25% vs 5%; OR 7.50, 95% CI 4.27–13.17), mostly neuro/motor (weakness, cramps, gait) and sensory/pain (back, limb, muscle pain; median about 1–2 years before onset); trembling of arms or legs was the most frequent specific complaint (OR 3.24). More neurology visits before onset (OR 1.26), more herniated discs (14% vs 7%, OR 1.85; 68% occurred 5+ years before onset; stronger in men and spinal onset), more physically demanding jobs (OR 2.21) and lower secondary education (OR 1.93), more past (not current) caffeine, alcohol and smoking. Men with ALS lost weight before symptom onset (time-by-sex interaction P = 0.009); at survey, ALS weighed about 6.4 kg less than controls. Limitations: recall bias, retrospective design, controls mostly relatives, no clinical/biomarker/EMG validation, incomplete age data, many comparisons (some findings did not survive FDR correction). Associations only; not causal and not predictive. Summary based on the full text (Europe PMC open access).
