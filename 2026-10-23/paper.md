## One-line note
In HeLa/HEK293 cells and in reconstituted G3BP1–poly(A) RNA condensates, the authors show stress granules are sites of elevated hydrogen peroxide (APEX2–Amplex UltraRed and HyPer7/HyPer3 sensors), and that minimal G3BP1–RNA condensates generate H2O2, hydroxyl and carbon-centred radicals without enzymes (NMR, EPR, single-molecule HPF imaging). Multi-bait BioID found peroxiredoxins (PRDX1, 2, 3, 6) recruited near stress granule proteins under arsenite stress; PRDX1, 2 and 6 partition modestly into granules. Blocking the peroxiredoxin–thioredoxin system (auranofin, conoidin A, adenanthin) made granules more oxidizing, slowed and oligomerized TDP-43ΔNLS (FCS, FRAP) and drove its demixing/aggregation inside granules (median G3BP1–TDP-43 correlation 0.88 → 0.30 with auranofin). N-acetyl cysteine (10 mM, in cells) prevented aggregation; tethering PRDX1/2/6 to G3BP1 reduced aggregation (PRDX1 strongest), whereas catalytically inactive PRDX1(C52S/C173S) and TRX1 did not. Limitations: preprint; cell lines and in vitro condensates only (no neurons, animals or patients); overexpressed cytoplasmic TDP-43ΔNLS and pharmacological stressors; HyPer readouts partly reflect reductive capacity; the authors state their data do not establish condensation itself as the ROS source in cellular stress granules.
